            How this is calculated

            Each line is one state's average of every reporting auction, averaged by head, for the same calves, lots and cull grades as the rest of the page (see "How these numbers are calculated" at the bottom). A cull cow or bull line is drawn only where the state sold at least 20 head in two-thirds of its sale weeks (weekly) or months (monthly). The week still under way is left off the weekly chart; the latest month can be partial. Circled points rest on fewer than 20 head. Individual auctions aren't compared here: one auction's sales are too few for a fair comparison.

                How this is calculated

                Each bar shows how far that month's price usually runs above or below the average of the 12 months around it, averaged over the years shown. Measuring against the months around it keeps a rising market from making the late months look better than they were. Changes in the grade mix are taken out too. Thin lines on each bar show the lowest and highest year. A heavy weight group with too few sales for a pattern uses the nearest lighter group's pattern.

                About these numbers

                Prices come from USDA Agricultural Marketing Service auction reports.

                How these numbers are calculated
                • Feeder prices are Medium and Large frame calves graded #1, #1-2 and #2, in dollars per hundredweight (cwt), from regular lots. Regular lots include calves reported as unweaned: some states' reporters label them and others don't, so counting them keeps regular lots the same in every state. Value Added lots (groups the reporter marks as Value Added, usually weaned or preconditioned calves that typically sell at a premium) are kept separate: pick Value Added at the top of the page to see them where there are enough sales. Fancy, Fleshy, Thin Fleshed and other described lots, and #3 calves, are left out. Cull cows and slaughter bulls are Average dressing. Prices are averaged by head; USDA's report averages are weighted by pounds, so the two can differ by a few cents.
                • The statewide view combines every reporting auction in the state. Graded, special and board sales are not included.
                • Market reporters grade and price part of each sale (often 40-90%), and prices here come only from the cattle they priced; the About box on This week gives that share. Likely reporting errors are left out, such as a price more than three times, or less than a fifth of, the national typical price for similar cattle that month.
