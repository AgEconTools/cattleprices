        Calculation methods

        Average dressing, all three cull grades, the last three sale weeks. Value per head is the price times the average weight sold. The change from a year ago is figured at today's average weight, so a lighter or heavier mix of cows isn't counted as a price change.

        Seasonal trend Statewide

        Calculation methods

        Each bar is how far that month's price usually runs above or below that year's normal level (a centered 12-month average), averaged over the years shown. The long-run price trend is removed first, so a rising market doesn't make the late months look better than they were, and the grade mix is held fixed. Thin lines on each bar show the lowest and highest year.

        About these numbers

        Prices come from USDA Agricultural Marketing Service auction reports.

        How these numbers are calculated
        • Feeder prices pool Medium and Large frame calves graded #1, #1-2 and #2, weighted by head, in dollars per hundredweight (cwt). Only plain lots count: #3 and Value Added, fancy and fleshy lots are left out. Cull cows use Average dressing.
        • The statewide view combines every reporting auction in the state. Pick one auction to see its own prices. Graded, special and board sales are not included.
        • Market reporters grade and price part of each sale (often 40-90%, depending on the auction). Prices here come only from the cattle they priced; the "About this sale" box on This week gives that share for the market you pick. Small weight classes can rest on a handful of head, so head counts are shown. Likely data-entry errors are left out.
